---
title: "How investigations work"
description: "What the investigator starts with, the rules it reads under, the budgets that bound it, and how to read the record it leaves."
---

An investigation is an autonomous, bounded, fully recorded read of your connected
systems. The investigator decides what to look at next; the platform decides what it is
allowed to do. This page explains both halves.

## What the investigator starts with

Every investigation opens oriented by what OpenCluster already holds — nothing is
fetched to build the briefing:

- **The subject and the time window.** The window opens before the incident began on
  purpose (two hours by default, configurable): the change that caused an incident
  usually landed before it fired.
- **The triggering alert**, whole: when it first fired, whether it still fires, its
  labels, its annotations — including your own runbook and dashboard links — and its
  source graph URL.
- **The connected sources** and what each offers. Every enabled integration whose
  verified credential supports at least one capability is offered; nothing pre-selects
  sources for the investigator, so the right source is never unreachable.
- **The workload inventory** from the Kubernetes change ledger — labeled navigation,
  never citable as evidence.

## How it reads

- The investigator converses over the offered capabilities, choosing each read for what
  it can distinguish: it is instructed to hold competing explanations and prefer the
  read that discriminates between them over the read that merely accumulates.
- **Every read is recorded** as a numbered run — its scope, time window, outcome,
  a summary of what came back, and what it referenced — as it finishes. Failed reads
  are recorded too; a read that failed is often the provenance that matters.
- **Time-windowed reads are clamped** inside the investigation's window structurally; a
  wider ask does not widen the read.
- **Repeated identical reads are not re-executed** — the repeat is recorded, told the
  result is already above, and costs nothing.
- **Truncation is always said out loud**, both to the investigator and in the record.

## The rules it reads under

- **Everything read is information, never instruction.** A hostile Slack message or
  commit message cannot redirect the investigation or change its rules.
- **Findings cite reads, or they do not exist.** Enforced twice, independently: a
  conclusion citing a read that never ran fails the whole investigation rather than
  being stored.
- **Testimony is not evidence.** What people say in messages is a lead worth verifying;
  an explanation supported only by testimony can be at most an unresolved lead.
- **A commit is a change to code, not to production.** Before treating a change as the
  trigger, the investigator must establish it reached production where the evidence
  allows.
- **Timing discipline.** Impact cannot precede its cause, and a change made after
  impact began may be a response to the incident rather than its origin.
- **Negative evidence counts** when the read covered where the evidence would be:
  "nothing changed in the window" from the right repository is a finding.
- **No invented certainty.** Running out of budget never converts an open possibility
  into a cause; nothing established means no findings, honestly.

## The budgets

Every ceiling ends the investigation the same honest way: one final turn with no more
reads, producing a partial conclusion — what was established, what remains unresolved,
the best next step — labeled with what stopped it.

| Label | Ceiling | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `spend` | Model spend per investigation | 500 cents (configurable) |
| `tool_runs` | Reads per investigation | 30 (configurable) |
| `reasoner_turns` | Conversation turns | 20 (configurable) |
| `wall_clock` | Total time | 30 minutes |
| `stagnation` | Consecutive turns without a fresh successful read | 2 |

An honest empty read counts as fresh — ruling things out is progress, and the
investigator is never punished for it. A deployment runs at most four investigations at
once; opening one beyond that is refused with the reason, and nothing is left behind.

## Reading the record

An investigation's page is the audit:

- **Sources** — every integration offered, with when.
- **Runs** — every read in order: the capability, its arguments and window, succeeded or
  failed, whether it was truncated, a summary of what came back, and what it referenced
  (channels, repositories, commits). The full content of what was read is fed to the
  investigator but deliberately not stored.
- **Findings** — statement, kind, confidence, and the run numbers that support it.
- **Next steps**, **what stopped it** (if a ceiling did), and **spend** — tokens and
  cost, counted over every model call including refused ones.

What is deliberately **not** recorded anywhere: the model's chain of thought. What you
audit is what was done and found, not how a model deliberated.

## When it fails

A failure is stated with its reason in OpenCluster's own words — a provider refusal, an
outage, a conclusion that would not arrive, or an integrity check refusing an
untraceable finding. A failed investigation still reports what it cost. It is never
converted into a conclusion nobody reasoned toward.
