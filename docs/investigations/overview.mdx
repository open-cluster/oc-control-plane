title: "Investigations"
description: "Open an investigation on an incident and get a causal, cited, auditable conclusion — not a restated symptom."
---

An investigation answers the question an on-call engineer actually has: **what is
happening, why, and what should I do about it**. OpenCluster's investigator reads across
your connected integrations autonomously — deciding what to look at next the way an
experienced responder would — and concludes with findings that have causal structure,
stated confidence, and citations back to every read that supports them.

## What a conclusion contains

**Findings**, each labeled with its causal role:

| Kind | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Probable cause | What initiated the incident. Several are legal — an incident with several causes is reported as one |
| Triggering change | The deployment or edit that set it off |
| Contributing factor | Made it worse or let it spread |
| Symptom | A visible effect, not an explanation |
| Propagation effect | Damage arriving downstream of the cause |
| Ruled out | An explanation that was checked and excluded |
| Unresolved lead | A plausible explanation that could not be checked, and what read would settle it |

Each finding carries a **confidence** — `confirmed` (the cited reads establish it),
`likely` (supported, while a plausible alternative remains), or `possible` (one open
explanation among several) — and cites the numbered reads that support it. A finding
without a citation cannot exist: it is refused before it is stored.

**Next steps**: recommended actions an operator can execute, ordered by reversibility —
rollback before configuration change before code fix before infrastructure change.

**The full record**: every source consulted, every read with its scope and outcome and
what came back in summary, and what was spent — so any claim can be audited down to the
read it came from.

## Opening one

- **From an incident**: open an investigation on any open incident episode.
- **By asking**: pose a question in plain words — "investigate checkout latency". If it
  ties to exactly one open incident, the investigation opens; if it matches none or
  several, you get one clarifying question back and nothing opens.

Opening needs the **Editor** or **Admin** role (it spends model budget and reads your
systems); reading investigations needs only **Viewer**. A deployment without a model
provider configured refuses to open investigations and says so.

## The three outcomes

| Outcome | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Concluded** | The investigator finished and stands behind the conclusion. Zero findings is a legal conclusion — it means the reads established nothing, honestly |
| **Concluded, stopped by a limit** | A budget (spend, reads, turns, time, or stagnating reads) ended the reading early. The conclusion is real but partial: what was established, what remains unresolved, and the best next step — labeled, never dressed up as a completed diagnosis |
| **Failed** | The investigation could not run — a model provider refusal or outage, or an integrity check refusing an untraceable finding. A failure is never rendered as a conclusion |
