An investigation reads connected sources to answer an operational question or investigate an incident. It has a time window and execution limits, so the answer reflects the evidence available for that turn.

A completed investigation can answer a factual question, support an explanation, or remain inconclusive. Completion alone does not mean a cause was established. Missing access, telemetry, or human input may prevent a defensible answer.

## Conversations and follow-ups

Keep related questions in the same conversation. For example, ask “Why did checkout latency rise?” and then “Does the deployment at 09:42 explain it?” Earlier cited findings provide context without changing the earlier result.

Follow-ups open new investigations. Messages sent while work is active wait and may be handled together in the next turn. Conversation history provides context, but it is not a complete memory or fresh evidence. Ask explicitly for a new interval or a fresh check when conditions change.

In Slack, mention the app again in the same thread. Slack evidence reads stay in that thread, while other eligible connected systems can supply evidence. See [Slack access boundaries](/integrations/collaboration/slack#read-slack-as-evidence).

## Findings and citations

Findings describe observations, causes, contributing factors, or explanations that the evidence ruled out. A causal finding explains how something produced the impact. An unconfirmed explanation remains a hypothesis, and limitations identify missing evidence or unresolved assumptions.

Each citation points to a Tool Run, a record of an attempted read from a connected source. Findings must cite successful reads. Failed reads can explain a limitation but cannot establish a finding.

Check the source, timestamps, requested interval, and incomplete-result indicators before relying on a finding. A citation shows what OpenCluster read, without guaranteeing that the source was complete or correct. Reused evidence keeps its original time and source reference.

## Inspect a cited observation

Editors and Admins can inspect the retained source observation behind a citation using the [observation API](/api-reference/investigations/inspect-one-retained-tool-observation). Viewers can read summaries and Tool Run metadata, but cannot retrieve these observation bodies.

Observations are bounded and may omit source content. Older observations can be unavailable. See [response details](/api-reference/investigations/inspect-one-retained-tool-observation) for fields and [Security](/security/overview) for sensitivity and retention.

## Action Proposals

An action proposal recommends mitigation, rollback, verification, a permanent fix, or monitoring. It explains the rationale, how to verify the result, and the supporting evidence. OpenCluster does not execute it.

For example, a rollback proposal may cite a workload event and a commit, then describe how to check latency and error rate afterward. Review the proposal and its evidence before making a production change.

## Postmortems

After an incident resolves, an Editor or Admin can request a draft postmortem through the [Generate postmortem operation](/api-reference/postmortems/generate-postmortem). It combines the incident's alerts, investigation findings, conversation testimony, and explicit human input. Generation is not automatic.

Missing impact, resolution, owner, or deadline facts appear as `Needs human input.` You can correct and regenerate the draft before marking it reviewed. Review records the reviewer, and the postmortem is not automatically published. Human testimony remains distinct from system evidence.

See [Run your first investigation](/getting-started/run-your-first-investigation) for the question-to-answer workflow and the [API reference](/api-reference/conversations/list-conversation-turns) for time windows and turn pagination.
